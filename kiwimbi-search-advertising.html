Nonprofit Search Advertising: Building Kiwimbi's Google Ad Grant Program

At a glance

  • Organization Kiwimbi International, a U.S. nonprofit that partners with Kiwimbi Kenya to run free public libraries and education programs in western Kenya
  • Role Marketing Analyst Intern (remote), Feb 2024 – Jul 2025; built and managed the search advertising program
  • Budget Google Ad Grant, up to $10,000/month in search ads
  • Goals Awareness (including fundraising events in U.S. cities), volunteer recruitment, donations
  • Tools Google Ads, Google Analytics 4, Google Tag Manager
  • Results +46% website traffic year over year; click-through rate raised from a 9.19% campaign average to 12.29%, more than twice the grant's 5% minimum; the volunteer campaign was paused after a surge in applications

The starting point

Kiwimbi had a Google Ad Grant but no one to run it. There were no campaigns, no conversion tracking, and no data on who visited the website or what they did there. I taught myself Google Ads and built the program from scratch.

The grant comes with constraints paid advertisers don't face. Accounts must keep a click-through rate of at least 5% every month and must track conversions, and grant ads struggle to compete on popular, high-volume searches. Results had to come from precision, not spend.

Building the campaigns

I organized campaigns around the organization's three goals: awareness of its work and its fundraising events in U.S. cities, volunteer recruitment, and donations. From there, the work was continuous refinement.

Keywords. Removed low performers and tested new and trending terms, focusing on specific searches that grant ads could realistically win.

Ad copy. Rewrote and tested ads to better match what people were searching for.

The website. Worked with my supervisor and the IT lead to speed up page loads by optimizing images, add search keywords to page content, and update parts of the site. Google factors landing-page experience into ad quality, so the site work fed directly into campaign performance.

Measuring what visitors did

Using Google Tag Manager, I set up tracking for 8+ key actions across the site, from clicks on the home page, newsletter, and volunteer pages to completed donations, and connected it all to Google Analytics 4. Donations were the hardest to track and took the most troubleshooting.

The tracking showed what brought people to Kiwimbi: learning about the work, volunteering, giving, or attending an event. I built KPI dashboards that leadership used to follow traffic, engagement, and conversions. The tracking also met the grant's conversion requirement.

Results

+46% website traffic in the program's first year, compared with the year before.

12.29% click-through rate, up from a 9.19% campaign average after a round of changes aimed at it, and more than twice the grant's 5% minimum.

Donations, sign-ups, and events. The ads drove donations and newsletter sign-ups, and brought supporters to Kiwimbi's events in U.S. cities, including charity runs.

More volunteers than needed. Volunteer applications grew to the point that we paused the volunteer campaign.

What I took from it

On a fixed budget with strict rules, the gains came from the details: tighter keywords, better ads, and a faster site. Measuring actions rather than clicks is also what told us when the volunteer campaign had done its job.
