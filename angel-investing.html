Venture Capital Due Diligence: Making the Case for Auxilium Health

At a glance

  • Fund Northeast Ohio Student Venture Fund, which invests in early-stage Northeast Ohio startups, through Wooster's partnership with the fund
  • Role Analyst on a 4-person deal team; diligence lead on Auxilium Health
  • Pipeline 15 applications screened → 4 in full diligence → 1 led
  • Method Pitch deck and financial review, market sizing, revenue projections, valuation, on-site lab review, founder and scientist sessions
  • Outcome The fund invested in Auxilium. The company has since raised $4.9M across two oversubscribed rounds, plus a $275K NSF grant.

The pipeline

The fund screened 15 early-stage applications that semester. The first pass was about fundamentals: what problem each company solves, whether the market actually needs it, and who it competes with. Our deal team then took four companies into full diligence: pitch decks, financial projections, market sizing, revenue and production-cost assumptions, and valuation.

Leading the Auxilium diligence

Auxilium Health is developing a biomaterial wound-care platform built on its founders' doctoral research at the University of Akron. On paper, it was the hardest company in our set to evaluate: the value depended on whether the science held up, and a pitch deck can't answer that.

My science background made me the natural lead. I visited their lab and met the founders and scientists to understand the technology, the value proposition, where production stood, and what scaling it would take. That firsthand look shaped our conclusions more than anything in the deck.

The investment thesis

I believed Auxilium was the strongest opportunity in our set, for three reasons.

A large, painful problem. Chronic, hard-to-heal wounds affect millions of patients, and undetected infections carry serious consequences.

Founder–science fit. The technology came straight out of the founders' own research and had been validated through the National Science Foundation's I-Corps program.

A credible path from lab to product. Seeing the production setup in person made the scale-up plan concrete rather than theoretical.

Making the call

Auxilium wasn't the consensus pick at first; some of the team leaned toward other companies. I made the case for it, and the team backed the recommendation. We presented it to the fund's board, alongside the college's president, deans, and faculty.

Outcome

The fund invested. Since then, Auxilium has closed an oversubscribed $1.5M pre-seed round, above its $1.2M target, and secured a $275K NSF grant (source).

It then closed an oversubscribed $3.4M seed round to move its platform toward FDA clearance (source).

What I took from it

The most important diligence happened outside the deck. Going to the lab is what turned a hard-to-judge science bet into a conviction I could defend in front of a board.
