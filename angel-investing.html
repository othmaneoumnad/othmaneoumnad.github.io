Angel Investing: Startup Due Diligence & Recommendation

At a glance

  • Focus Startup due diligence and investment recommendation for an active venture fund
  • Program ENTR 405 (Angel Investing), College of Wooster, in partnership with the Northeast Ohio Student Venture Fund
  • Role Evaluated four startups from the fund's applicant pool, Atmata, Co2mposites, Pigybak, and Auxilium, leading the diligence on Auxilium
  • Method Founder meetings, a lab visit, market research, and financial modeling
  • Headline Ranked Auxilium as the top recommendation. The company has since closed an oversubscribed $1.5 million pre-seed round.

The program

The Northeast Ohio Student Venture Fund partners with student teams across several Ohio universities to evaluate early-stage founders applying for funding. Each semester, student teams run full due diligence, product, market, team, and financials, and present a formal recommendation to the Fund's board, which then makes the actual investment decision.

The assignment

Our team evaluated four companies from that semester's applicant pool: Atmata, an early-stage robotics startup building a low-cost robotic camera-stabilization system for filmmakers; Co2mposites, a sustainable materials startup developing a CO2-based resin for lightweight composite products; Pigybak, a contractor-booking marketplace, post a job, compare quotes, chat, and book, that also lets nearby homeowners pool their requests together for group pricing and fewer wasted trips; and Auxilium Health, a biomaterial startup building next-generation wound-care technology.

I led the diligence on Auxilium. As an economics major, the technical depth wasn't my background, but I understood the underlying science better than anyone else on the team, which put me in charge of it.

What the work looked like

On Auxilium, I ran the market research, met with the founders directly, visited their lab, and worked through the financial modeling myself, the full scope of the diligence, not just one piece of it. Seeing the technology in person and asking the founders direct questions about how it actually worked, rather than relying on the pitch deck alone, shaped the recommendation more than anything else. The team also traveled to Akron to hear pitches from other companies in the Fund's pipeline in person, useful for seeing how founders held up their numbers under direct questioning.

The call

The recommendation was a clear ranking, not four equal endorsements.

Auxilium was the top pick. Chronic, hard-to-heal wounds affect millions of people and carry real health consequences when infections go undetected. The founders, both PhDs out of the University of Akron, were building on technology that came directly out of real dissertation research, validated through the National Science Foundation's I-Corps program. The lab visit and direct conversations with the founders made this the strongest case in the pool.

Pigybak ranked second. The core product solves a genuine, everyday problem: finding and booking a contractor is usually a scattered mess of phone calls and texts, and Pigybak put that whole process, quotes, chat, scheduling, in one place. The group-pricing layer on top of that was the sharper insight: a crew is often paying for the trip more than the job itself, so pooling nearby homeowners into the same booking lowers the price for homeowners and fills out the route for contractors. The founder had also lived the problem locally, in Northeast Ohio, rather than studying it from a distance, which came through clearly in the pitch.

Co2mposites and Atmata tied for third. Several teammates favored Co2mposites, and I leaned slightly that way as well, but neither company made a compelling enough case to recommend over the other.

What happened since

Auxilium Health has since closed an oversubscribed $1.5 million pre-seed round and secured a $275,000 NSF grant. Pigybak is also still active and has since been featured in Forbes and won a 2025 industry innovation award. Two of my top two picks holding up years later is a genuinely satisfying piece of validation for a call made as an undergraduate. The outcomes for Co2mposites and Atmata aren't known, due diligence work like this is a snapshot in time.
